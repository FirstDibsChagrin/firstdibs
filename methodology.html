Methodology

How the Cuyahoga County competition risk score is built — sources, formulas, and limitations.

What the score measures

The competition risk score (0–100) estimates how difficult it is for a regular financed buyer — using a mortgage, needing inspection contingencies, and competing on offer price — to successfully purchase a single-family home in a given ZIP code. A higher score means more competition from institutional and cash buyers, faster-moving markets, and less affordability headroom.

Six market signals are combined using a weighted average. Each signal is normalized to [0, 1] before weighting so all factors contribute on the same scale. When a factor is unavailable for a ZIP, its weight is redistributed proportionally among the remaining factors, keeping the score meaningful as long as at least 50% of the total weight is covered.

The six factors

Factor 1 — Corporate / LLC Ownership %

Estimated   Weight: 22%

What it represents: The estimated share of residential parcels in each ZIP held by corporate or institutional owners — LLCs, holding companies, REITs, and similar — rather than resident homeowners. A higher share indicates more competition from investor buyers.

This factor is an estimate, not a measured value. The intended source is the Cuyahoga County Fiscal Officer parcel database, matching deeded owner names against a corporate/LLC regex (LLC, INC, CORP, HOLDINGS, REALTY, PROPERTIES, VENTURES, INVESTMENTS, CAPITAL, EQUITY, FUND, PARTNERS, LP, LLP, REIT, excluding government and land-bank owners). That parcel service was not reachable from the build environment, so per-ZIP values were instead estimated from published investor-concentration research — Reinvestment Fund reports, Cleveland Neighborhood Progress housing data, and CWRU Center on Urban Poverty studies — calibrated so the east-side urban core reads roughly 28–36% and affluent east suburbs roughly 6–9%. The values are directionally reasonable for ranking ZIPs but should not be read as exact county figures. Recomputation instructions (runnable in about 15 minutes from an unrestricted network) are in data/cuyahoga_methodology.md.

Normalization: 5% → risk = 0; 40% → risk = 1.

Factor 2 — Median Days on Market

Real data   Weight: 12%

Rolling 90-day median days-on-market for homes sold, sourced from Redfin's ZIP-level market tracker (MEDIAN_DOM column). Fewer days = hotter market = higher risk.

Normalization: 60 days → risk = 0; 5 days → risk = 1.

Factor 3 — Sale-to-List Ratio

Real data   Weight: 12%

Average ratio of final sale price to original list price for homes sold in the rolling 90-day window (AVG_SALE_TO_LIST). A ratio above 1.0 means homes sold above list price, indicating bidding-war conditions.

Normalization: 0.95 → risk = 0; 1.05 → risk = 1.

Factor 4 — % Sold Above List

Real data   Weight: 12%

Share of homes that closed at a price above their list price in the rolling 90-day window (SOLD_ABOVE_LIST, stored as a fraction in the Redfin file, converted to percent). A high share signals persistent bidding-war activity.

Normalization: 0% → risk = 0; 60% → risk = 1.

Factor 5 — Price-to-Income Ratio

Workaround sources   Weight: 30%

Redfin median sale price divided by ACS median household income. Higher ratios mean homes are less affordable relative to local incomes, which concentrates demand among cash buyers and high-income earners.

Home value: Redfin MEDIAN_SALE_PRICE (All Residential, 90-day rolling window ending March 2026). Zillow ZHVI was the intended source but was inaccessible.

Income: ACS 5-year 2012–2016 (2016 inflation-adjusted dollars), from the public GitHub repository Ro-Data/Ro-Census-Summaries-By-Zipcode. The Census ACS API was inaccessible. The 2016 income data inflates ratios by roughly 20–30% compared to 2022 ACS values — use this factor for relative comparisons between ZIPs only, not as an absolute affordability measure.

Normalization: 2× → risk = 0; 8× → risk = 1. ZIPs with extremely low 2016 incomes in mixed commercial areas (e.g., 44114 Downtown, 44115 Midtown) produce outlier PTI values above 10× due to the income vintage mismatch.

Factor 6 — Months of Supply

Derived from real data   Weight: 12%

Estimated months of housing inventory. Redfin no longer publishes this directly at ZIP level, so it is derived as: INVENTORY ÷ (HOMES_SOLD ÷ 3), where HOMES_SOLD covers a 90-day window (dividing by 3 gives a monthly sales rate). Below 3 months indicates a seller's market; above 6 months indicates a buyer's market.

Normalization: 8 months → risk = 0; 1 month → risk = 1.

Composite score formula

Each factor value is first normalized to [0, 1] using the anchors in the table above:

norm(v, lo, hi) = clamp((v − lo) / (hi − lo), 0, 1)

Then the weighted average is computed over factors with available data:

score = round( Σ (norm_i × w_i) / Σ w_i × 100 )

ZIPs where the sum of available factor weights is below 0.50 (50%) are flagged as having insufficient data and are not scored.

Adjustable weights

The score is a weighted average of the six factors. The map page exposes four preset configurations and a custom slider panel so users can tune the score to their situation. Changing weights does not change the underlying data — only how the six factors are combined.

Weights are entered as integers (raw slider values). At compute time they are divided by their sum, so they automatically normalize to 100% regardless of what values are set. This means users can drag sliders freely without factors fighting each other.

When to use each preset

  • Default — balanced starting point for a general first-time buyer using financing.
  • Affordability first — emphasizes the price-to-income ratio; best for lower-income buyers or FHA-likely borrowers who need to stretch the budget as little as possible.
  • Investor-aware — heavily weights corporate/LLC ownership; useful when the primary concern is competing against cash-buying institutional buyers.
  • Speed of competition — weights time-on-market and bidding signals most heavily; best for cash-ready buyers who can move fast and want to identify the most frenetic markets.

Custom weights

The "Customize weights" panel on the map page exposes one slider per factor. Drag any slider from 0 to 100 — the normalized share bar underneath each slider shows what percentage of the total that factor currently represents. The score auto-updates live as you drag. Custom settings persist across page loads via localStorage (key: firstdibs.weights.v1).

ZIP universe

51 Cuyahoga County ZCTAs — sourced from scpike/us-state-county-zip on GitHub (based on US Census 2000 ZCTA geography). The authoritative 2020 Census ZCTA-to-county relationship file (tab20_zcta520_county20_natl.txt) was inaccessible during data collection.

Boundary ZIPs that straddle county lines (e.g., 44022 Chagrin Falls, 44040 Gates Mills) may have changed county assignments since the 2000 Census; treat these as approximate. The 51 ZIPs are consistent with the known Cuyahoga ZCTA list.

Known limitations

  • Income data vintage: The price-to-income ratio uses 2016 ACS income data paired with 2026 home prices. This inflates PTI values by approximately 20–30% across all ZIPs and produces extreme outliers for ZIPs with small residential populations in commercially-dominated areas (44114, 44115). Use PTI for relative ZIP ranking, not absolute affordability comparisons.
  • Months of supply derived: Computed from inventory and homes-sold counts rather than Redfin's published months-of-supply field (which is null at ZIP level). More volatile for low-volume ZIPs.
  • Redfin sample size: Four ZIPs (44040, 44114, 44115, 44127) have fewer than 10 homes sold in the latest 90-day window and use a trailing 3-month median instead.
  • Corporate ownership is estimated, not measured: Factor 1 is the one input not computed from a primary source. The Cuyahoga County Fiscal Officer parcel service was unreachable during the build, so per-ZIP values were estimated from published investor-concentration research and are directionally reasonable but not exact county figures. Treat them as a ranking signal, not a precise measurement. A recomputation from the ArcGIS parcel service is documented in data/cuyahoga_methodology.md and can be run in about 15 minutes from a normal network environment.
  • Score is relative, not predictive: The risk score measures observable market conditions; it does not predict whether a specific offer will succeed. Use it to prioritize neighborhoods for research and to calibrate offer strategy.

Help you can apply for

The Help you can apply for sidebar card shows programs that may help cover your down payment or improve your loan terms. Programs are drawn from data/cuyahoga_assistance_programs.json, which was populated from publicly available program guides and annual reports.

FHA numbers (ZIP-specific)

The FHA Originations and FHA Denial Rate rows display data sourced from the FFIEC Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) LAR file (2023 vintage). These figures reflect actual lending activity for the selected ZIP code, so they vary by ZIP.

  • FHA originations — count of FHA purchase loans originated in the ZIP in 2023.
  • FHA denial rate — share of completed FHA purchase applications denied. A high denial rate signals that lender criteria or property conditions may be a barrier in this market.

Other programs (county-wide or statewide)

All other programs — VA, OHFA, FHLB Welcome Home, Cuyahoga DPA, Cleveland HOME, Land Bank, and Habitat for Humanity — are listed as eligibility catalogs. They do not show ZIP-level activity because reliable address-level records were not publicly available at collection time.

  • OHFA (Ohio Housing Finance Agency) — county-level data from the OHFA FY2024 Annual Report (Appendix B). Cuyahoga County: 460 loans totaling $80.3M (avg $174,510) in FY2024. Statewide: 4,488 loans.
  • Cleveland HOME DPA — activity from the City of Cleveland CAPER FY2023–2024. 21 households received direct financial assistance; $440,615 HOME funds disbursed. This program is shown only for ZIPs in the city of Cleveland (44102–44120, 44127–44128).
  • FHLB Welcome Home — program guide only; no ZIP-level activity data available. 2023 available funds: $2.8M statewide. Grant maximum: $20,000 ($25,000 for veterans).

Affordability calculator

The Can I afford here? calculator takes five inputs — annual income, cash saved, monthly debts, credit score range, and preferred loan type — and estimates the maximum home price a buyer could qualify for under FHA and/or conventional financing. All math is client-side; no data is sent anywhere.

This is an estimate, not a pre-approval. Lenders consider your full credit history, employment history, assets, and the specific property. Use this number as a rough starting point, then talk to a lender or a free HUD-approved counselor for an actual figure.

FHA vs conventional — what's different

FHA loans are backed by the Federal Housing Administration and accept lower credit scores (580+ for 3.5% down, 500–579 for 10% down). They require Mortgage Insurance Premiums (MIP): an upfront fee of 1.75% of the loan rolled into the balance, plus an annual fee of 0.55% of the remaining loan divided by 12 and added to each monthly payment.

Conventional loans are not government-backed. They typically require a 620+ credit score. First-time buyer programs (Fannie Mae HomeReady, Freddie Mac Home Possible) allow 3% down. Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) applies when the down payment is under 20%; the calculator uses a mid-credit estimate of 0.50% annually. PMI can be removed once equity reaches 20%.

How the maximum price is calculated

The calculator solves for the home price P two ways and takes the lower.

1. Income constraint (back-end DTI): Max monthly housing payment = (annual income ÷ 12) × DTI cap − existing monthly debts. The monthly payment includes principal & interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, and MIP or PMI. The equation is linear in P and solved exactly — no iteration needed.

2. Cash constraint: Available cash must cover (minimum down payment % + 3% closing costs) × price. Max price from cash = available cash ÷ (down % + 3%).

The calculator reports which constraint bound the result ("Reason for the cap"). The result is rounded down to the nearest $1,000.

ZIP affordability buckets

Each ZIP is classified against the FHA maximum price (or conventional if FHA is not selected) using the Redfin median sale price as the home-value proxy:

  • Affordable — median price ≤ max price × 0.95 (5% headroom for rate fluctuation and negotiation).
  • Stretch — median price ≤ max price × 1.10 (within 10% over budget).
  • Out of reach — median price > max price × 1.10.
  • No data — no recent median sale price available for this ZIP.

The "Both" map view layers affordability bucket fills (green = affordable, light green = stretch, gray = out of reach) over risk-level stroke colors so counselors can see competition and fit simultaneously.

Updating assumptions when rates change

All assumptions live in a single ASSUMPTIONS constant at the top of the <script> block in index.html. Update fha_rate_apr and conv_rate_apr each quarter using the most recent Freddie Mac PMMS 30-year fixed rate. Note the update date in a comment next to the value.
