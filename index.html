Beat the bid toolkit
Escalation Clause
An add-on to your offer that automatically beats competing bids, up to a cap you set.
What it is

If another buyer submits a higher offer, your bid automatically increases by a set amount — say $1,000 — until you hit your ceiling. Then it stops. You stay competitive without going over your limit.

When to use it

High-competition ZIPs with short median days on market and many homes selling over asking. Good when you want to stay in the running but still have a firm spending ceiling.

When NOT to use it

When the market is slow (median DOM over 30 days) or when your financing is tight — if the escalated price pushes past the appraised value, your lender may not cover the gap.

Ask your agent: "Can we add an escalation clause that beats any competing offer by $1,000, up to a maximum of $[X]?"
Appraisal Gap Coverage
Promise to cover the difference if the home appraises for less than your offer.
What it is

Lenders only finance up to the appraised value. If you offer more than that, you cover the difference in cash. Writing this into your offer tells the seller you won't walk away if the appraisal comes in low.

When to use it

When homes regularly sell over list — sale-to-list ratio above 1.05 or more than 40% of homes selling above asking. Common in Very High and Extreme competition ZIPs.

When NOT to use it

If you don't have extra cash available after your down payment and closing costs, don't commit to a gap you can't cover.

Example

Home listed at $250,000. You offer $275,000. Appraisal comes in at $260,000. With $15,000 gap coverage written in, your offer holds. Without it, your lender only finances against $260,000 and the deal can fall apart.

Ask your agent: "What's a reasonable gap-coverage amount for offers in this market right now?"
Full Underwriting Pre-Approval
Pre-approval where the lender has fully checked your finances, not just a soft credit pull.
What it is

A standard pre-approval letter is a lender's estimate. A fully underwritten pre-approval means an underwriter has actually reviewed your income documents, bank statements, and employment — and conditionally approved your loan. Sellers know the difference.

When to use it

Always, in any market. Especially important when few FHA buyers are winning offers — a fully underwritten letter signals you're as close to a cash offer as a financed buyer can get.

Note

Takes longer than a standard letter — sometimes 1–2 weeks. Plan ahead before you start making offers.

Ask your lender: "Can you do a fully underwritten pre-approval — sometimes called a TBD approval or upfront underwrite?"
Down Payment Assistance
Money to help cover your down payment, sometimes forgivable after a few years.
What it is

Federal, state, and local programs that provide money for your down payment and/or closing costs. Many don't have to be repaid if you stay in the home for a set number of years.

When to use it

Any first-time buyer stretching to reach a down payment. Income limits often apply but are higher than people expect — typically 80% of area median income, which in Cuyahoga County is roughly $75,000–$86,000 depending on household size.

Find a Free HUD-Approved Counselor
Government-certified homebuyer counseling agencies — free to you.
What it is

HUD-approved housing counseling agencies must meet federal standards and charge nothing for basic homebuyer counseling. A counselor will review your budget, explain your loan options, identify programs you qualify for, and help you build a plan.

Why it matters

Most people don't know this service is free. Counselors can help you figure out DPA eligibility and what offers you can realistically make — before you fall in love with a home.

How the risk score works

Six market signals — corporate ownership, days on market, sale-to-list ratio, % sold above ask, months of supply, and price-to-income ratio — are each normalized and weighted.

Score 0–100 — higher means more competition from cash and institutional buyers. Weights renormalize automatically when a factor is missing for a ZIP.
